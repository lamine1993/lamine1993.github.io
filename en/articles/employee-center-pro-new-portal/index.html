Building a new Employee Center Pro portal without touching production

Employees use the portal every day, so you cannot rebuild it live. Here is how I built a new Employee Center Pro portal next to the old one, then prepared a cutover with no downtime.

By Mohamadou Lamine SarrOctober 22, 20267 min read

At Desjardins, I worked on the advanced configuration of Employee Center Pro (ESC), the single portal where employees find their services, requests and knowledge articles.

The challenge: deliver a new experience (new structure, new theme, new menus) without disrupting the portal everyone was using. The answer was to build a second portal in parallel.

Why a parallel portal

Changing the production portal directly causes three problems:

  • every taxonomy or menu change is immediately visible to all employees;
  • you cannot have a pilot group test the new version;
  • rolling back is hard once several changes are mixed together.

A parallel portal solves all three: the old one keeps running, the new one is built at its own pace, and the cutover becomes a single, controlled decision.

Step 1: create the new portal

In ServiceNow, a portal is a record in the sp_portal table, with its own URL suffix, home page, theme and menu. The new portal gets a suffix different from the old one. Both live on the same instance and share the same catalog and knowledge base.

Tip

Never modify the out-of-the-box Employee Center pages and widgets. Copy what you need to change and attach the copy to the new portal. Platform upgrades stay easy to apply.

Step 2: structure the taxonomy

The taxonomy is the portal's map: the main topics (for example "My workplace", "HR", "Access and security") and their subtopics. Employees browse by subject, not by department.

I structured the new portal's taxonomy, then attached content to each topic: catalog items, knowledge articles, quick links. One simple rule helps a lot: an employee should find what they need in three clicks or fewer.

Step 3: create a theme with the Branding Editor

The Branding Editor lets you build a theme without writing CSS: logo, colors, typography, banner image. I used it to create a custom theme for the new portal, in the organization's colors.

The advantage over hand-written CSS: the theme stays compatible with future Employee Center releases, and a non-technical team can adjust it later.

Step 4: customize the header and widgets

Header and menus

The header is the most viewed part of the portal. I added new contextual menus that show different links depending on the employee's profile, using user criteria. Everyone sees what concerns them first.

My Favorites and My Applications

I enabled and configured two widgets employees love: My Favorites, to reach frequently used services in one click, and My Applications, to open company applications straight from the portal.

Step 5: test and switch over

The parallel portal makes it possible to test in real conditions, with no risk:

  1. Tests by profile: by impersonating different users, we check that each one sees the right menus and content.
  2. Pilot group: a group of employees uses the new portal and gives feedback before everyone else.
  3. Cutover: when ready, the new portal becomes the default landing page. The old one stays available for a while, so we can roll back in minutes if needed.

Key takeaways

  • Build alongside, not on top. It is the best way to ship a big change without disrupting anyone.
  • Taxonomy matters more than design. A beautiful portal that is badly organized is still a bad portal.
  • Stay close to the baseline. Branding Editor, user criteria and out-of-the-box widgets: less custom code means simpler upgrades.
